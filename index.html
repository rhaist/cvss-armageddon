How this is counted (and why you should still read the advisory)

Source: the CVE List V5 (CVEProject/cvelistV5 on GitHub). Included are records first published in the chosen window of UTC days (6–19 November 2026 at most), up to the snapshot time shown at the top. The last day was still going at snapshot time, so its late hours are empty, not quiet. Drupal is missing on purpose: it rates advisories on its own 25-point scale, and the CVE records for this month's Drupal advisories (such as CVE-2026-126725) had not been published to the CVE List at snapshot time.

Score: the highest CVSS base score in each record, from the CNA or from CISA's ADP enrichment. The list mixes CVSS 3.1 and 4.0, which are different scales, so a 9.3 in 4.0 and a 9.3 in 3.1 don't mean the same thing. — of the records in this window had no score at all yet; NVD scoring usually lags, so some of those may turn critical later.

Blast radius: a tier per product from 1 (under ~1k deployments) to 5 (millions), set from public install or internet-exposure counts where they exist (W3Techs, Censys, Shodan, watchTowr, BuiltWith, WordPress.org, ecosyste.ms), an order-of-magnitude judgement for well-known products, and no tier at all for the long tail. It measures how many systems might run the product, not how many are vulnerable: many of these bugs need a specific configuration, and internet-exposure counts undercount internal installs.

Not a measure of exploitation. Check CISA KEV and the vendor advisory before you wake anyone up.
